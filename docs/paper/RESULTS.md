Columns add up: `inst` = `n_gm` + CMSGen=0 + GPAP=0 + both=0 + no-search + tiny-space + vacuous. Every one of those instances is counted exactly once, `wins/n` is counted over the same set the geomean is (plus the two zero columns), and the degenerate cells are screened out BEFORE the ledger rather than only out of the geomean.

**Two rate columns, and each charges BOTH sides the same way.** `GPAP/CMSGen` is steady state over steady state: GPAP's winning-config sampling rate over CMSGen's samples per SAMPLING wall (its own `--statsevery` clock, which starts after it has read the CNF). `e2e` is end-to-end over end-to-end: GPAP's `wall_total` (parse + engine construction + ELS + warmup + entailed-clause MINING + the timed run) over CMSGen's whole subprocess wall. `pre%` is the median share of GPAP's end-to-end wall that preparation takes. Earlier revisions scored GPAP's steady state against CMSGen's subprocess wall, which charged one side for getting ready and not the other. Preparation is a real cost at these `n_time` values and does not amortise away until the run is much longer — quote `e2e` for a delivered-samples claim and the steady-state column only for the large-n regime, saying which. The two protocols are DIFFERENT TASKS and must never be pooled: 'their pins' rows pin the 1–5 outputs the rival's own table names (rejection-easy by construction — p_accept is 0.03–0.5 there, printed per row), while miter/rare rows pin ALL outputs to a rare reachable pattern (p_accept ~1e-2–1e-6). Recurrent-CircuitSAT is scored on its own depth-POOLED unique count — solutions satisfying the target at ANY unroll depth start_point..T, a superset of the fixed-depth task every other row measures (the fixed-depth-only count is recorded per row as `unique_at_T`). **The batch a rival is handed is its score**: measured, both are near-proportional to it over the whole range the card will run (DEMOTIC 564x from 1k to 1M), so it is the same knob `K` is for us. Each rival is therefore granted the LARGEST rung of that ladder it can run to completion: the ladder is walked DOWN from the top and a rung is rejected only on feasibility — out of memory, refused by the driver, or not finished inside the wall budget — never on how fast it was, so exactly ONE configuration is measured per instance. (Until 2026-09-04 the walk started at our VRAM model's rung, which is calibrated at num_steps=10 while we run 1 and so under-granted DEMOTIC 1.5-3.9x on 4 of 11 ISCAS-85 cells, and a timeout was recorded AS the rival's result.) The first run of every process is discarded before anything is measured, because a cold CUDA context costs the session's first instance ~3.3x. Each rival is handed an ABC-simplified copy of the circuit as well as the raw one and is scored on whichever suits it. The rungs tried are recorded per row as `batch_sweep`, the resolved budget as `vram_budget_bytes`. Their own defaults cannot serve as the rule — all three argparse defaults are `--batch_size 10` and their demo scripts are per-instance one-liners spanning 1,000 to 1,000,000 on comparably tiny circuits, which on our instances would hand them a small fraction of their achievable throughput and inflate the ratios below by the same factor. **Their `--num_steps` and `--lr` are theirs, not ours**: the batch is a share of the machine and must be granted, but the gradient-step count and the learning rate are tool settings with author-chosen values, so each rival runs at the ones its OWN artifacts specify — `num_steps` 1 and `lr` 15 for both (their `script/run_sampler_*.sh`). Where a tool ships two, the tie-break goes to whichever is better FOR THE RIVAL, which can only move a ratio against us — Recurrent's paper text names `lr` 50 where its script says 15 — and it is recorded per row as `steps`/`steps_source` and `lr`/`lr_source`, re-derived on the campaign's own card by `eval/rivals/steps_calibration.py --axis steps|lr`. It is deliberately not swept, since a swept-and-best-of setting invites "why stop there?". Rows recorded on or before 2026-08-26 pinned 10 for both, which taxes DEMOTIC 2.6x and Recurrent 8.5x (both saturate their batch on the first step) and is why the DEMOTIC and Recurrent rows here are far below their previously recorded values. Rival rows recorded before 2026-08-25 used a fixed 5 GiB budget and are not comparable to rows from the current harness. Constraint alignment is VERIFIED per row: miter mode asserts p(MITER=1) equals the instance's p_accept; native mode asserts bit-identical acceptance words between their index table on the original file and our reordered pins (`pin_check_ok`). **No tool is timed while delivering its solutions**: CMSGen runs under the patch's `--nowrite`, DEMOTIC counts and dedups its solutions after its own timer stops, and our timed run gathers no model row to the host (`collect=False`; the rival arm keeps EVERY delivered row as GPU-projected support bits and deduplicates them exactly, off the clock, the same quantity both rivals report — the dedup runs after the sampler returns and is timed as `distinct_s`, and the projection wall that gathered the rows is subtracted from the steady-state AND the end-to-end rate, so measuring distinctness cannot pay for itself out of the number it measures) — re-derived from the pinned sources by `eval/rivals/check_emission_symmetry.py`. The one exception is Recurrent-CircuitSAT, which copies its per-depth candidate assignments to the host inside its timed loop: the share of its `model_runtime` that costs is bounded per row by the audit script (section 4 of its report — quote the campaign's figure, it depends on the card's transfer rate) and left uncorrected so its rate is exactly the one it publishes. Deducting it would RAISE the ratios below, so those rows under-report GPAP rather than flattering it.

## Table 4b — Barbarik CM_test (the standard certificate)

`eps=0.3 eta=1.8 delta=0.1` — the parameters CMSGen's own authors certify at (FMCAD'21 Table I): 36 rounds x (1 + 1 + 50) = **1,872 samples** for an Accept, per-round accept band **[0.05, 0.95]**. An Accept means *not caught being eta-far* (L1 >= 1.8, i.e. TV >= 0.90) and holds only under Barbarik's non-adversality assumption; a Reject is unconditional. It is not a proof of uniformity — the exact-oracle fuzzing campaign (docs/findings/UNIFORMITY_FINDINGS.md) is the sensitive instrument.

65 eligible instances (3 dropped because the trusted reference is itself Rejected on them — a non-independent sampling set, or a delta-level false rejection of an exactly uniform sampler; either way the test cannot speak about the instance; 0 dropped on a reference error).

## Caveats that MUST appear in the text

* `rivals:demotic_comb_rare/abc:fast`: 2 instance(s) (`c17`, `c432`) sample with **no search at all** — zero decisions, zero implications, success 1.0 — because after propagation the support is a free hypercube. Excluded for the same reason as vacuous pins. Leaving them in would read **213.6x instead of 229.7x**.
* `rivals:recurrent_rare/abc:fast`: 6 instance(s) defeated **both** samplers (`b15`, `b17`, `b20`, `b21`, `b22`, `s35932`) — a limit of the comparison, not evidence about either tool.
* Recurrent-CircuitSAT (their target table, $T{=}25$): **10 of 30** runs failed (did not initialize within the 300 s budget, timed out, or no gradient path). They are omitted from the geomean — instances the rival cannot process — never counted as zeros.
* Recurrent-CircuitSAT (our rare pins, $T{=}25$): **9 of 30** runs failed (did not initialize within the 300 s budget, timed out, or no gradient path). They are omitted from the geomean — instances the rival cannot process — never counted as zeros.
* GPAP/CMSGen ratios are **host-dependent**: CMSGen is single-core, and this host is 0.41x the reference laptop (verified independently by CMSGen's own propagation and decision counters). Always quote the CPU alongside the ratio.
* Sequential geomeans **exclude** `b02`, `b05`, `b07`, `b13`, `s526` (rivals:recurrent_table/abc:fast); `b02`, `b05`, `b07`, `b19`, `s526` (rivals:recurrent_rare/abc:fast) — cells whose pinned outputs are constant for every input sequence (measured p_accept 1.0), so pinning them is a tautology.
* What the degeneracy screens (vacuous pins, no-search and tiny-space cells) do to each geomean, in **both** directions — screened / unscreened: rivals:recurrent_rare/abc:fast 1.39x (the screens LOWER this headline); rivals:demotic_comb_rare/abc:fast 0.93x (the screens RAISE this headline); rivals:recurrent_table/abc:fast 0.89x (the screens RAISE this headline); rivals:demotic_native/abc:fast 0.84x (the screens RAISE this headline). A screen that raises a headline is reported here exactly as loudly as one that lowers it.
* **Where GPAP loses, and why**: every loss to CMSGen is a cell where propagation buys almost nothing over blind rejection AND absolute success is tiny — amplification (success / p_accept) at most 57.40x together with success at most 4.1e-03 (`b11`, `b12`, `b18`). No cell meets both conditions and still wins (0 counter-examples), so the boundary is descriptive rather than fitted. That is the rejection sampler's collapse regime, and it is where a CDCL sampler should be preferred.
* Cells where CMSGen delivered fewer than 30 samples in its whole budget — `b14` (13) — carry a ratio whose denominator is a handful of events. Read them as 'CMSGen could not sample this', not as a measured factor.
* Rare-target rival ratios rest on near-zero denominators and a single rival run against GPAP's 3-seed median: read them as orders of magnitude, not constants.
