## Table 1 — GPAP vs CMSGen

**One row per (family, encoder). Nothing selects between the encoders**: both pipelines a user could ship are reported side by side, because choosing per instance by the highest ratio would let CMSGen's number pick which of our runs is published (measured: it moves the sequential families 1.24-1.33x), and because the encoder choice is not monotone — ABC unlocks `b14` at a rare pin and collapses `b13`'s.

Columns add up: `inst` = `n_gm` + CMSGen=0 + GPAP=0 + both=0 + no-search + vacuous. Every one of those instances is counted exactly once, `wins/n` is counted over the same set the geomean is (plus the two zero columns), and the degenerate cells are screened out BEFORE the ledger rather than only out of the geomean.

**Two rate columns, and each charges BOTH sides the same way.** `GPAP/CMSGen` is steady state over steady state: GPAP's winning-config sampling rate over CMSGen's samples per SAMPLING wall (its own `--statsevery` clock, which starts after it has read the CNF). `e2e` is end-to-end over end-to-end: GPAP's `wall_total` (parse + engine construction + ELS + warmup + entailed-clause MINING + the timed run) over CMSGen's whole subprocess wall. `pre%` is the median share of GPAP's end-to-end wall that preparation takes. Earlier revisions scored GPAP's steady state against CMSGen's subprocess wall, which charged one side for getting ready and not the other. Preparation is a real cost at these `n_time` values and does not amortise away until the run is much longer — quote `e2e` for a delivered-samples claim and the steady-state column only for the large-n regime, saying which. The two protocols are DIFFERENT TASKS and must never be pooled: 'their pins' rows pin the 1–5 outputs the rival's own table names (rejection-easy by construction — p_accept is 0.03–0.5 there, printed per row), while miter/rare rows pin ALL outputs to a rare reachable pattern (p_accept ~1e-2–1e-6). Recurrent-CircuitSAT is scored on its own depth-POOLED unique count — solutions satisfying the target at ANY unroll depth start_point..T, a superset of the fixed-depth task every other row measures (the fixed-depth-only count is recorded per row as `unique_at_T`). **The batch a rival is handed is its score**: measured, every one of the three is near-proportional to it over the whole range the card will run (HTS 295x from batch 100 to 100k, DEMOTIC 564x from 1k to 1M), so it is the same knob `K` is for us. Each rival is therefore granted the same share of the card GPAP grants itself: the largest batch that fits in 0.80 of free VRAM, stepping down a rung if the driver refuses the size. The rungs tried are recorded per row as `batch_sweep`, the resolved budget as `vram_budget_bytes`. Their own defaults cannot serve as the rule — all three argparse defaults are `--batch_size 10` and their demo scripts are per-instance one-liners spanning 1,000 to 1,000,000 on comparably tiny circuits, which on our instances would hand two of the three ~1/300 of their achievable throughput and inflate the ratios below by the same factor. **Their `--num_steps` is theirs, not ours**: the batch is a share of the machine and must be granted, but the gradient-step count is a tool setting with an author-chosen value, so each rival runs at the one its OWN artifacts specify — DEMOTIC 1 and Recurrent 1 (their `script/run_sampler_*.sh`), HTS 10 (its `flags.py` default). Where a tool ships two, the tie-break goes to whichever is better FOR THE RIVAL, which can only move a ratio against us; it is recorded per row as `steps` / `steps_source` and re-derived by `eval/rivals/steps_calibration.py`. It is deliberately not swept: measured, HTS's optimum on these instances is at or above 20 and we run it at 10. Rows recorded on or before 2026-08-26 pinned 10 for all three, which taxes DEMOTIC 2.6x and Recurrent 8.5x (both saturate their batch on the first step) and is why the DEMOTIC and Recurrent rows here are far below their previously recorded values. Rival rows recorded before 2026-08-25 used a fixed 5 GiB budget (and HTS its own scripts' batch 100/1000) and are not comparable to rows from the current harness. Constraint alignment is VERIFIED per row: miter mode asserts p(MITER=1) equals the instance's p_accept; native mode asserts bit-identical acceptance words between their index table on the original file and our reordered pins (`pin_check_ok`). **No tool is timed while delivering its solutions**: CMSGen runs under the patch's `--nowrite`, DEMOTIC and HTS count and dedup their solutions after their own timer stops, and our timed run gathers no model row to the host (`collect=False`) — re-derived from the pinned sources by `eval/rivals/check_emission_symmetry.py`. The one exception is Recurrent-CircuitSAT, which copies its per-depth candidate assignments to the host inside its timed loop: measured at 0.7-1.4% of its `model_runtime` (bound <=2.2% on these cells), left uncorrected so its rate is exactly the one it publishes. Deducting it would RAISE the ratios below, so this row under-reports GPAP by ~1% rather than flattering it.
