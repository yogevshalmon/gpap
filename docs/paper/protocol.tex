\subsection{Benchmarks and protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Every ratio below depends on choices a circuit-sampling paper does not usually state: whose
circuits are sampled, which output pattern is pinned, what a fair baseline emits, on which
clock, and how a failure on either side is counted. This subsection fixes them once. Every
table that follows obeys them, and every number in those tables is produced by one script
from one campaign directory, so the rows of one table line up against the rows of the next
instance by instance.\footnote{\texttt{eval/run\_full\_eval.sh campaign}; the tables by
\texttt{eval/artifact/paper\_tables.py}, the publishability gate by
\texttt{eval/artifact/verify\_artifact.py}, which re-derives every check named here from the
raw rows.}

\paragraph{Benchmarks: the rivals' own.}
We evaluate on the circuits that the GPU circuit samplers we compare against publish results
on, not on a set of our choosing. \demotic{}~\cite{demotic} evaluates the 11 combinational
ISCAS-85 circuits~\cite{brglez_iscas85_1985}, \texttt{c17} to \texttt{c7552};
\recurrent{}~\cite{recurrent} evaluates sequential circuits from
ISCAS-89~\cite{brglez_iscas89_1989} and ITC'99~\cite{corno_itc99_2000}. Our sequential set is
the full ITC'99 suite, all 21 circuits \texttt{b01}--\texttt{b22} (there is no \texttt{b16}),
plus the 8 ISCAS-89 circuits its paper names. Its table lists an \texttt{s35844}, which is not an
ISCAS-89 circuit; the row's own figures (12 inputs, 278 outputs, 1{,}452 flip-flops) identify it
as \texttt{s38584}, and that is the circuit we run. \texttt{s35932}, the only \texttt{s359xx}
branch in its released code, is kept as a circuit \emph{we} add and is scored outside its set.
That is 30 circuits, each unrolled from reset at \emph{both} depths we can justify: 25 cycles,
and the 50 its own run script and paper use ($\eta = 50$, over which its sampler pools every
depth). The two are never pooled; a shallower unroll gives the rival a smaller pool to find
solutions in, so reporting only the shallower one would understate it. The primary inputs across
cycles are $\support$ (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}). \recurrent{}'s paper reports 16 of the 29; we score all 29 and
print the geometric mean over its own 16 beside every sequential number, because the two
differ and a reader should see by how much. Nothing else is added: no benchmark family of our
own, no second unroll depth, no second encoder. A third GPU sampler of the same
gradient-descent family, HTS, is not compared: its paper evaluates no circuit at all, so
every cell would be our task on instances we chose.

\paragraph{Two tasks per circuit.}
Each circuit is pinned in two ways, and the two are never pooled. Under \emph{their pins}, the
one to five output bits the rival's own run script names for that circuit are fixed to the
values it names, verbatim. Under \emph{our rare pin}, every last-cycle output is fixed to the
least frequent pattern observed in a $262{,}144$-sequence simulation batch (pattern-selection
seed fixed): reachable, hence satisfiable, but adversarial for rejection by construction. The
acceptance probability of every cell is measured by simulation and printed beside its row:
under their pins $0.02$ to $0.97$, with two cells (\texttt{b01} and \texttt{b19} under
\recurrent{}'s table) where no hit was observed in the batch, bounded below $10^{-7}$ and
$2\times10^{-5}$; under the rare pin from $0.5$ (\texttt{b01}, whose outputs take only two
patterns) down to $2\times10^{-8}$, twelve cells being an upper bound where no hit was
observed. A pin whose measured probability is $1$ is a tautology and is screened (below); there are five
such cells on the sequential set under each task. On a handful of circuits the \emph{rarest}
observed pattern is not rare at all --- some circuits produce only a few distinct output patterns
--- and those cells are reported with their measured probability beside them rather than
presented as adversarial. The rare task is what makes the comparison informative: bit-parallel simulation of
the circuit, keeping the input sequences that happen to hit the pinned outputs, is a perfectly
uniform sampler with no \cnf{} machinery, and at a typical reachable target it beats every
\cnf{} sampler, ours included; a circuit-sampling comparison means something only where
rejection fails. For the rivals, which consume circuits, the rare pin is expressed as a
single-output miter over the same netlist; \gpap{} and \cmsgen{} sample the pinned circuit
(the miter form on the sequential set, the directly pinned outputs on ISCAS-85), the same
projected solution set by construction. Identical solution sets do not make identical
\emph{tasks} for a gradient method, and we measure that rather than assume it: on the cells
where both pins are provably tautologies --- so the two forms have the same solutions, the whole
input space --- collapsing the pins into one output moves \recurrent{}'s rate by up to two
orders of magnitude in either direction. \gpap{}'s moves by under $1.5\%$ on three of those four
cells and by $4.3\times$ on the fourth, where its adaptive portfolio switches configuration on a
formula propagation has already solved. Part of what
the miter rows measure is therefore how a gradient sampler handles a deep comparator cone, and
the text says so. Constraint alignment is verified on every row of
every table, and a row that fails is excluded and counted: under their pins, pinning their
index table on the original file and our reordered outputs must accept bit-identical input
sequences under identical stimuli; under the rare pin, the miter's acceptance probability must
equal the instance's, within simulation tolerance.

\paragraph{Three tools, one constraint.}
Every (circuit, task) cell is run by three samplers on the same constraint. The rival takes
the circuit itself, raw, and unrolls it its own way, in one configuration that is its
author's: the step count and learning rate its own run script specifies (\texttt{num\_steps}$=1$ and
\texttt{lr}$=15$ for both; where a tool ships two values for a knob --- \recurrent{}'s paper
text names $50$ for the learning rate --- the tie-break goes to whichever is better for the
rival, re-derived on this card), the largest rung of a $\times 3$ batch ladder that it can
actually run to completion (the rivals' throughput is near-proportional to their batch, so the
grant is the score: the ladder is walked \emph{down} from the top and a rung is rejected only
when the tool cannot finish on it --- out of memory, refused by the driver, or past the wall
budget --- never on how fast it was, so exactly one configuration is measured), and its own
default random seed, passed explicitly and recorded, at which it is run \emph{once}: the rival
is measured in one configuration, and the asymmetry against \gpap{}'s median of three seeds is
stated here rather than hidden. Each run is given a 300\,s wall, and \recurrent{} a further
300\,s for the \texttt{start\_point} initialization its loss requires. That budget is ours, not
theirs, so a cell where the tool exhausted it at every batch rung is recorded as bounded by our
wall rather than by its capability, and counted apart from a failure. The first run of every process is discarded before anything is measured: a
cold \textsc{cuda} context costs the first instance of a session roughly a factor of three, and
without that warm-up the cost lands on whichever circuit is measured first. Nothing is swept, and a
run that times out, finds no gradient path, or does not initialize is recorded as such and
omitted from the geometric mean with the count stated, never scored as a zero; those are the
largest circuits, so the geometric mean is over the rival's easier half, and the tables say so.
\gpap{} takes the same circuit and pins, optimized by \abctool{}~\cite{brayton_abc_2010} (its
fast script, after unrolling) and Tseitin-encoded~\cite{tseitin_complexity_1983} over the
optimized netlist. The rivals are not left holding the raw netlist for that reason: each is run
on both the committed circuit and an \abctool{}-simplified copy of it (equivalence-checked
against the original under identical stimuli), and the table scores, per instance, whichever of
the two is better for the rival. The post-unroll pass still has no sequential equivalent, which
is stated rather than hidden. It samples with
the shipped defaults: a 60\,s sampling budget and a $50{,}000$-sample target, the median of
three seeds, and the adaptive two-configuration portfolio (random variable order, with 8
decisions per round or with 1; both uniformity-safe). That portfolio is the one
asymmetry in \gpap{}'s favour: the rivals and \cmsgen{} each run one fixed configuration,
while \gpap{} measures its two against each other inside the timed wall and reports the
winner's rate. We report the adaptive default because it is what ships, and state the asymmetry; the price of
pinning it to one configuration is measured on the campaign's own rows and reported with them
(the figure previously quoted here came from a smaller sweep under a different encoder and
budget, and does not transfer).
\cmsgen{}~\cite{cmsgen} receives that very \cnf{} file, with the same target count and a
60\,s sampling budget of its own, in one run at its default seed. Everything after encoding,
mining, simplification and the engine, is inside \gpap{}'s end-to-end clock; encoding is
charged to no sampler and reported per instance.

\paragraph{Metrics and clocks.}
Against the GPU rivals we use their metric verbatim: unique solutions per second of their own
\texttt{model\_runtime}, a timer that excludes their model build. \gpap{}'s comparable figure
is the winning configuration's steady-state sampling rate, which likewise excludes parsing,
construction, simplification, mining and warm-up, times the distinct fraction of everything the
timed run delivered --- every row, deduplicated exactly. That is the same quantity, computed the
same way, as the rivals': \recurrent{} deduplicates its whole candidate set per depth and
\demotic{} its whole solution list once its timer has stopped, both outside their own clocks, and
ours is likewise outside both of ours. The deduplication runs after the sampler returns, and the
wall of the projection that gathers the rows is subtracted from the steady-state \emph{and} the
end-to-end rate, so the measurement of distinctness cannot pay for itself out of the number it
is measuring; both walls are recorded per row. The same table prints, beside the steady-state ratio, the
end-to-end one, \gpap{} charged its whole wall against the rival's unchanged timer.
\recurrent{} is further credited with solutions at any unroll depth
up to 25, a superset of the fixed-depth task every other row measures. Against \cmsgen{} we
compare raw samples per second, the deliverable both produce, on two clocks that each charge
both sides alike: \emph{steady state}, \gpap{}'s sampling rate against \cmsgen{}'s samples per
second of sampling wall on its own counters, and \emph{end to end}, \gpap{}'s whole wall from
parsing through mining, warm-up and the timed run against \cmsgen{}'s whole subprocess.
Neither side is deduplicated in that comparison: both tools emit repeats where the solution
space is small relative to the request, so cells whose estimated solution count is below
$1{,}000$ are screened from both tables (below), and \gpap{}'s measured distinct fraction is
recorded per row: $1.000$ to three decimals on 65 of the 73 cells that kept rows; the eight
below it are the tiny-space cells screened above (4 to 829 solutions) and two single repeats
among the $1{,}000$ rows (\texttt{c1908} under its pins and \texttt{c432} under the rare
pin, $0.999$). No tool is timed while
delivering solutions: stock \cmsgen{} formats and writes every sample as a full
$\nvars$-variable assignment as it goes, so we patch it to generate without writing and to
stop itself at the budget with an exact count (a killed run reports a floored one);
\demotic{} counts its solutions after its timer stops; \gpap{}'s timed run gathers no model to
the host. The one exception is \recurrent{}, which copies candidate assignments to the host
inside its loop, bounded on this campaign's rows at 0.1--15\% of its \texttt{model\_runtime}
(30\% on one cell under a pessimistic transfer-rate assumption; the audit script prints the
bound per row) and left uncorrected in its favour.
Soundness is checked twice on every row: every model \gpap{} emits is re-checked on the
device against every clause of the formula, and the kept output rows are simulated through
the pinned netlist itself (the original circuit, or its miter form on the sequential rare
task) and must reproduce the pinned outputs, a check no encoding or preprocessing step can
fool. Both self-tests are outside every rate. \gpap{} and the GPU
rivals run on one NVIDIA L4 (24\,GB); \cmsgen{} runs on one core of the same host's Intel Xeon
at 2.20\,GHz, recorded with every row, because \cmsgen{} is single-core and any ratio against
it is partly a statement about the host.

\paragraph{Accounting.}
A cell that measures the instance rather than a sampler leaves every geometric mean through
its own column, detected mechanically per row and before any zero is counted: a
\emph{vacuous} pin (the pinned pattern is a tautology, measured acceptance probability $1$), a
\emph{free} cell (propagation alone fixes the support, so sampling is a random-bit readout: no
decisions and no implications, or fewer than two implications per solution delivered --- on our
rows that separates one degenerate cell from a nearest neighbour forty times above it), a
\emph{tiny} space (an estimated solution count below $1{,}000$, the smallest of the input
hypercube $2^{|\support|}$, the measured acceptance probability times it, and the count implied
by the repeat rate over the delivered rows; both tools enumerate such a space rather than sample
it, the exception \demotic{}'s own table makes for \texttt{c17}), and a cell \emph{proved
unsatisfiable} at the depth it was measured at, where neither sampler can produce a solution
because none exists. Zeros
are then handled the same way on both sides: no cell where either sampler produced nothing
enters a geometric mean, and every table reports the baseline-zero, \gpap{}-zero and
both-zero counts, which sum with the screened cells and the geomean's instances to the cell
count, so the ledger can be checked by addition. Excluding the baseline's zeros is
conservative: those cells are outright wins. A refusal on our side, a formula outside the
engine's packed envelope, is a reach limit and is counted apart from a zero. Uniformity is a
separate claim, made in Section~\ref{sec:uniformity} with Barbarik~\cite{barbarik} on circuits
from the same families under our pins, with an exactly uniform reference put through the same
hook as the negative control and a known-biased configuration as the positive one.
