# WAVE vs SCALAR propagate kernel — the L4 A/B (2026-08-23)

**Question**: how much faster is the warp-per-row WAVE propagate than the serial-trail SCALAR
kernel, as a function of instance size — the number the paper states beside the dispatch rule
(`cpp/src/calibrate.cpp::gpap_dispatch_wave`: WAVE iff M ≥ 2048).

**Answer (NVIDIA L4, Colab, torch 2.11/cu12.8, 2026-08-23)**: **WAVE / SCALAR = 1.30× geomean
over 93 cells (median 1.36×, WAVE faster on 81/93)**, rising monotonically with clause count —
**1.01× below 2k clauses, 1.33× at 2k–20k, 1.48× at 20k–100k, 1.64× above 100k** (WAVE faster on
every one of the 25 cells above 20k). Tables: `docs/paper/KERNEL_AB.md` / `kernel_ab.tex`
(generated by `eval/artifact/kernel_ab_tables.py`); raw data
`$GPAP_RESULTS/l4_campaign_20260823/kernel_ab_l4/kernel_ab.json`.

This is the same experiment as the RTX 4080 calibration recorded in CLAUDE.md (forced-kernel A/B
at fixed K: M=2,098 → 1.04, M=2,882 → 1.41, M=14,012 → 1.57, 1.27–1.66 for every larger M,
whole-set geomean 1.39–1.46 over 25 instances) — now at 141 cells, five families, both encoders,
on a second GPU, with a validation phase. The bins agree with the 4080 curve; the whole-set geomean
is lower only because this set holds many more small-M cells (the ABC-collapsed circuits), where
the two kernels tie.

## Method (`eval/kernel_ab.py`, driver `eval/colab/colab_kernel_ab_l4.sh`)

GPAP only. 141 (instance, encoder) cells: ITC'99+ISCAS-89 T=25, ISCAS-85 T=1 one pin, EPFL T=1,
HWMCC'17 T=25 — each with `tseitin` and `abc:fast`, `rare` targets — plus the 29 sk_ CNFs. Per
cell:

1. **Validation before timing.** Both kernels forced in turn via `$GPAP_KERNEL`, at a pinned
   K (`k_cap=4096`, so the cap binds and K is identical), fixed chunk count (3), seed 5. Required:
   gpu_sound on both, the sampler's own `engine.mode` provenance equal to the forced kernel, and
   **identical model counts** (BCP is confluent ⇒ same K/seed/chunks ⇒ the same models; the repo's
   no-change gate). **141/141 passed.**
2. **Timing.** Same process, same encoded CNF, pinned `b8` config (no bandit), **mining off**,
   20 s wall × 2 reps with the arm order alternated, best-of per arm; K recorded per arm
   (identical on 99/99 comparable cells). Ratio = wave/scalar.
3. Exclusions, as the paper tables apply them: 8 vacuous-pin cells, 5 zero-search cells (no
   decisions, no implications — the kernel is not what is being measured), 30 cells where neither
   kernel produced a model in 20 s (17 EPFL, 10 sk, 3 seq — no finite ratio), and a ≥100-models
   floor on both arms (6 cells; their ratios rest on a handful of models — the max "5.98×" is one
   of them). All 99 comparable cells: 1.33×; with the floor: 1.30× — the floor costs nothing.

Why each rule exists (each was a wrong number once, see docs/BENCHMARKING.md):

- a free bandit picks differently per arm ⇒ the A/B measures the bandit;
- mining is wall-budgeted ⇒ a faster kernel mines more clauses ⇒ the arms run DIFFERENT formulas;
- SM clocks drift within a run ⇒ un-alternated before/after cannot resolve a few percent;
- at small M a tiny `--n-time` finishes in one chunk and measures first-chunk overhead. 22 cells
  reached the 2M-model quota in <2 s (8 chunks); re-measured wall-bound (n_time=1e9, 20 s) they
  agreed with pass 1 **within ±3% on every cell** — the A/B is stable from a 0.3 s to a 20 s
  window at 8+ chunks. The wall-bound rows are the ones in the table (`remeasured_wallbound`).

## Results

| clauses M | cells | WAVE/SCALAR geomean | median | min–max | WAVE faster |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < 2k | 20 | 1.01× | 1.05× | 0.63–1.51 | 13/20 |
| 2k–20k | 48 | 1.33× | 1.37× | 0.80–1.69 | 43/48 |
| 20k–100k | 17 | 1.48× | 1.47× | 1.23–1.80 | 17/17 |
| > 100k | 8 | 1.64× | 1.64× | 1.36–1.88 | 8/8 |
| **all** | **93** | **1.30×** | **1.36×** | 0.63–1.88 | **81/93** |

Per family: sequential 1.36× (25/25), HWMCC 1.48×, sk_ 1.44× (17/17), ISCAS-85 1.18×, EPFL
1.16×. Per encoder: tseitin 1.33×, abc:fast 1.22× (smaller formulas), CNF 1.44×. The largest
gains are the 8|24 giants (b14 tseitin M=1.05M 2.03×, bobsynthor M=1.15M 1.88×, b14 abc 1.88×);
the largest losses are small EPFL/ISCAS cones (router tseitin M=802 0.63×, priority abc M=1,713
0.67×, c432 abc M=371 0.78×).

**The dispatch rule picked the faster kernel on 77/93 cells.** All 16 disagreements lie at
M = 549–8,726, the band where the kernels are close; the costliest mis-pick runs at 0.66× of the
faster kernel (epfl/cavlc abc:fast, M=1,851, scalar picked where wave was 1.51×), most are within
±10%. The M ≥ 2048 threshold is therefore about right on the L4 as on the 4080; a finer rule would
buy a few percent on a handful of small cones and is not worth a second calibration constant.

## Caveats to state with the number

- The IMPLICATION counters of the two kernels differ by ~0.6% on conflicted rows (which clause
  empties first depends on propagation order); models and decisions agree exactly — gate on
  models, as the validation phase does.
- Mining is OFF in this A/B; the shipped sampler mines by default, so the absolute rates here are
  not the headline rates — only the ratio is the result.
- Absolute rates on low-success instances are draw-noisy (±40%); hence the model floor.
- Cross-GPU: the bins agree with the 4080 calibration, but only the L4 numbers are tabulated here.
